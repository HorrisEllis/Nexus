A way of thinking, built into code you can test

James Brooks wrote down how he reasons: start from the end state, measure before you judge, treat constraints as a field, and read what is missing. Then he specified a system that does each of those, and directed it into code with tests that anyone can run.

Five methods, five systems

Each one: his words, what the system does, where the code is, and the tests that show it working.

1 · End-state-first reasoning → RAID

cortex/core/raid/*: contract intake, officiator, router, constitution gate, decision record
"Set the end-state → determine invariants → replicate conditions → test, iterate, loop."His Innovation Toolkit, Section 2

RAID turns a request into a contract, and the contract is the end state. Every step an agent takes is checked against that contract, and the officiator verifies the result against the real end state, not against the agent's own report. An agent cannot quietly redefine "done". Intent flows both ways: forward from the goal, and backward from the result. The test IF-05 shows this backward check refusing a change that forward-only planning would have accepted.

2 · Systems thinking → RFR2

intelligence/rfr2/delta → rfr2/sigma → causality → snapshot-trigger.js
"Invariants here = two+ points in deviation (measured in sigmas); the gaps between measured in deltas."His Innovation Toolkit, Part 3

Measurement and judgment are kept apart. Deltas only measure and have no thresholds. Sigma is the layer that judges, sorting behaviour into regimes: stable, degraded, oscillatory or collapsing. When the regime changes, a snapshot is taken and traced back to the conditions that caused it, so any state can be explained and reverted.

3 · Constraint field runtime → CFR

intelligence/cfr/field.js, lib/emerge/compiler/gap-field-engine.js
"Probabilistic probability on top of deterministic probability = a constraint field … the boundary of the constraint field = the signal. Everything else is noise, assumption, or convention dressed as necessity."His Innovation Toolkit, Section 3

Hard rules are the deterministic floor, and models work inside the space those rules leave. Wherever a rule is broken, the engine names a gap automatically. The gaps are derived from the rules, never written by hand. The field measures coherence and friction continuously instead of answering pass/fail.

4 · Reading between the lines → Gap Hunter and the Liminal Space reader

intelligence/gap/hunter.js, intelligence/liminal/ (12 detectors), intelligence/liminal-space/ (5 interstitial spaces)
"Gap hunter and liminal space reader is my ability to read in between the lines. A way to account for ambiguity. Shadow space, negative space, reasoning… Looking for what's not there to fill in… the in between the lines."James, 2026-11-04

Most systems analyse what is present. This one also looks at what is absent. The twelve detectors carry his names for it: negative space, shadow, contrastive, oscillatory, reversal, assumption, structural, relational, existential, field, code and music. The liminal-space organ watches the boundaries between layers, for example the threshold where intent meets constitution, and the membrane where a pattern meets its cause. Ambiguity is kept and named; it is never silently resolved.

5 · Grounded intuition → the crystallizer

intelligence/index.js _groundedCrystallise, intelligence/adversarial.js
"One gap = anomaly. Two = coincidence. Three = an invariant (not a fact)." · "Intuition must be regulated and grounded."His Innovation Toolkit, Part 3

A pattern is announced only when it passes three checks: it occurred at least three times, it occurs more often than the base rate would predict, and its type is not known noise. A pattern that fails is held as a hypothesis, which matches his "not a fact". Tests F2 and F3 show the difference this makes. A naive rule ("seen three times = a pattern") would announce a loud coincidence or a burst. His rule refuses both.

Where the code does not yet match the framework

On 2026-11-04 each part of his framework was checked against the code. These are the gaps found. They are listed because a portfolio that hides its gaps is not credible.

A real mismatch: f(slope). In his framework, slope pays off only up to a point ("the closer to unstable without destabilizing yourself, the more novel the solution"), so leverage should rise with slope and then turn down before instability (the coder's reading of his f(slope)). In friction.js, leverage keeps rising with slope and never falls. Fixing this needs the threshold, which is his to set.
Not built. His Priority formula, (Efficiency + Effectiveness) / Urgency, and his Weighting formula, Urgency × Importance × Fidelity × Slope. His Section 0 gate, "Is this even a framework problem?". The idea vectors for convergence and divergence.
Partial. In his framework, Signal = alignment + fidelity + expansion. The SNR gate checks fidelity only.
