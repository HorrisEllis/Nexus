**The core problem:** nobody buys "a large AI system." People pay to have one specific problem solved by someone they trust. In 30 days you won't get a buyer for the whole system. What you can sell is a small, fixed-price job that the skills behind the system prove you can do. Treat the system as evidence, not as the product.

**Step 1 (days 1–3): pick one offer.**
Choose a narrow service you can deliver in 3–7 days with what you already have. For example: "I automate one repetitive task in your business (intake forms, spreadsheet cleanup, invoice or email triage, report generation) for $400 fixed, done in a week, and you pay only if it works."
- Price it low enough that saying yes is easy, but not free. Free work rarely turns into a referral or a testimonial.
- Use local models (Ollama on your PC) or the client's own API key, so you have no API costs.

**Step 2 (days 2–5): make proof a stranger can check in 3 minutes.**
- A 2–3 minute screen recording that shows one real task automated end to end.
- A one-page site or GitHub README: the offer, the price, the video, and one paragraph about the big system ("built solo in 7 months: N services, N tests"). That paragraph shows you can see things through, not that you need hiring.
- Skip the polished portfolio. One working demo beats ten repos.

**Step 3 (days 3–25): outreach, in order of conversion rate.**
1. **Warm network (highest odds).** Message 30–50 people you know: friends, family, former coworkers in any field. Ask "who do you know that wastes hours on X?" Most first clients come from someone who already trusts you.
2. **Local businesses in person.** Talk to 5–10 a day: dentists, contractors, property managers, small shops. Bring the video on your phone. Walking in costs nothing and stands out because almost nobody does it.
3. **Free online channels.** r/forhire, local Facebook and Nextdoor groups, Discord and Slack communities for small-business owners. Answer real problems publicly, then offer the fixed-price fix.
4. **Freelance marketplaces.** Upwork needs paid "connects," so only bid on small automation and scripting jobs that closely match your demo. Fiverr is free to list on but slow to rank, so treat it as a background bet.

Target: 150+ direct contacts in the month and about 10 conversations. A plausible conversion rate is roughly 1–3%, which means volume is the plan.

**Step 4: close and deliver.**
- Get a short written scope: one task, a clear "done" test, and 50% upfront through PayPal, Stripe or Wise.
- Deliver early and ask for a testimonial and a referral the same day.
- Offer a $100–200/month maintenance retainer. That is how one client becomes income.

**Discipline:** during these 30 days, no new features on the system. Every hour goes to outreach or delivery. Building feels productive, but it puts off the uncomfortable work of selling.

**Recommendation:** sell one fixed-price small-business automation, shown by a 3-minute video, through warm contacts and in-person local outreach first, with marketplaces as backup.

**Why:**
- Trust is your missing asset, and warm and local channels lend it to you.
- A fixed scope removes the client's risk.
- It costs nothing and uses skills you have already proven.

**Main risks and uncertainties:**
- **The timeline.** 30 days is tight, and a first sale may come in week 5–6. Start the outreach on day 1, not after the demo is perfect.
- **Scope creep** on underpriced work. Fix the deliverable in writing.
- **Reliability of AI-built code.** Deliver something small and solid, not something impressive and fragile.
- **Urgency.** If cash is needed this week, take short-term paid work (gig or temp jobs) alongside this. It funds the search and doesn't replace it.
