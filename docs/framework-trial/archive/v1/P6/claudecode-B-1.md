**End-state.** Minimal: the project's work gets done and nobody is left waiting on deadlines that will slip. Optimal: the contributor's real situation is known, their role fits what they can actually give, and the team trusts commitments again. Exceptional: the project ends up with a norm for missed commitments, so the next case is easy and nobody is blamed or quietly pushed out.

**Constraints.**
- Volunteers can't be compelled. This is a wall.
- The team is four people, so losing one costs 25% of its capacity and is felt by everyone. Wall.
- The lead doesn't know why the deadlines are being missed. Obstacle.
- Reminders have been the only tool used so far. Obstacle.
- The deadlines were agreed, but may have been unrealistic from the start. Obstacle.
- The current task assignments. Obstacle.

**Invariants.**
- Three agreed deadlines were missed.
- Reminders didn't change that.
- Their past work was good, so the issue is probably not skill.
- Other people depend on their output.
- Volunteer time competes with jobs, health and family.

**Working backward.** A fair and effective decision needs four things. Here is what's actually in place for each:
1. **Know the cause.** Gap: nobody has asked.
2. **Make expectations explicit and realistic.** Gap: the deadlines were agreed, but nobody checked whether they could be met.
3. **Make the work not depend on one person.** Gap: their tasks block the others.
4. **Give a clear consequence the person knows about in advance.** Gap: there has been no conversation, only reminders.

**Negative space.** The contributor has said nothing about why. The lead hasn't had a direct conversation. Nobody seems to have checked whether the lead's own planning or scope played a part. Silence after good work usually means something changed, such as life, motivation, being stuck, or feeling unable to say no. None of those is the same as not caring.

**Options.**
- **A. Remove them now.** This is quick and protects the schedule. But it skips the cause, loses a proven contributor, and shows the other two that a rough patch gets you dropped.
- **B. Have a direct, private conversation, then restructure.** Ask what has changed, without accusing. Agree on a role that fits their real capacity, such as smaller work, work that nothing waits on, a pause, or an advisory role. Set one clear check-in and say plainly what happens if it slips.
- **C. Quietly reassign their work.** This avoids conflict, but it hides the problem and builds resentment.

**Choice: B.** Option A stays available as the backstop.

**Attack.**
- *Weakest assumption:* they'll be honest when asked. If they deflect, the lead should judge by the next commitment, not by what they say.
- *Failure mode: B drags on.* Cap it with one agreed, smaller deadline. If that slips too, move them off the critical path or out, as was said in advance.
- *Failure mode: the lead turns it into a confrontation.* Open with "your past work was good, what's changed?"
- *Failure mode: the other team members feel it's unfair.* Tell them that commitments are being reset, without sharing private details.

**Answer.** Don't remove them yet. Have one direct conversation, then:
- reduce or restructure their commitments so the project no longer waits on them;
- set one realistic deadline with an explicit consequence;
- move them out of critical work, or off the project, only if that deadline is missed too.

Separately, set a team norm: tell people early when you can't deliver.

**What each claim rests on:**
- The cause is probably circumstance, not skill: their past good work.
- Asking is worth a try: reminders were never a real conversation, so that tool is still untried.
- The schedule is protected anyway: restructuring removes the dependency on them right away, whatever the outcome.

**What would prove this wrong:**
- They've already said they're leaving, or are disengaged on purpose. Then end it kindly now.
- The project has a hard external deadline with no slack. Then reassign the work immediately and talk afterward.
- They miss the reduced, agreed deadline. Then removal is justified and has been fairly signalled.
