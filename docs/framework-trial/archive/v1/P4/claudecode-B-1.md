**End-state.** Minimal: one client pays any amount (even $200) for delivered work within 30 days. Optimal: $1–3k fixed-price project plus a referral or repeat. Exceptional: a monthly retainer, which proves the work can be sold over and over.

**Constraints.**
- Walls: no job history, no budget, mid-range PC, 30 days, works alone.
- Obstacles: "no credibility" (can be shown through proof), "no audience" (direct outreach gets around it), "no paid APIs" (local models and free tiers), "the system is too big to explain" (sell one slice of it).

**Invariants.**
- Buyers pay to get a specific problem solved. They don't pay for a system.
- Trust comes from evidence they can check: a demo, a repo, a quick win.
- Small businesses always have tedious work they'd like automated.
- Someone who has never heard of you replies to direct, specific outreach far more often than to passive posting.

**Working backward.** A payment needs a yes. A yes needs a concrete offer at a low-risk price. That offer needs a named problem and visible proof. The proof needs a demo someone can see in under two minutes. And all of that needs ~100 targeted conversations. Compared with reality, the gaps are no offer, no packaged demo and no pipeline. Skill is not a gap: seven months of shipped code and thousands of tests prove it.

**Negative space.**
- There is no one-sentence statement of what they sell.
- There is no niche.
- There are no testimonials yet.
- There is no clear price.
- Nobody has looked at the work. The silence so far comes from not being seen, not from being rejected.

**Options.**
- A. Freelance platforms (Upwork and similar): open bidding on small automation and scripting jobs. It's fast but crowded, and with no reviews they will lose on price.
- B. Direct niche offer: pick one vertical (e.g. local trades, clinics, e-commerce shops) and offer one fixed-price fix, such as "I automate your intake/quote/report process in 7 days, $500, pay on delivery." Outreach by email, LinkedIn and local business groups, with a loom-style demo.
- C. Open-source credibility: publish part of the system and wait for inbound work. Too slow for 30 days.

**Choice: B as the main track, with A running alongside** to fill the pipeline and earn a first review.

**Attack.**
- Weakest assumption: that they can turn a builder's mindset into a seller's one-liner and send outreach every day. Repair: write the offer before writing any code, and set a daily quota of 15 messages, tracked in a spreadsheet.
- Failure mode: they keep polishing the big system instead of selling. Repair: freeze the system. Only work that serves a client demo is allowed.
- Failure mode: pricing too high with no proof. Repair: pay-on-delivery or a 50% deposit, and ask for the testimonial as part of the deal.
- Failure mode: the niche doesn't bite. Repair: if fewer than 3 replies come from the first 75 messages, switch niche on day 10.

**Plan.**
- Days 1–3: pick the niche. Build one 2-minute demo video and a one-page site with the offer, the price and links to the repo and tests.
- Days 4–20: 15 targeted messages a day, plus 5 platform bids. Take every call.
- Days 10–25: deliver the first job fast and over-communicate.
- Days 25–30: get the testimonial, ask for referrals, and offer a monthly maintenance retainer.

**What it rests on.** Cold outreach reply rates of 2–5% (industry norm, so ~300 messages gives 6–15 conversations). Small-business demand for automation. The proof the developer already has.

**What would prove it wrong.**
- Zero replies after 150 messages: the offer or the niche is broken, not the plan's logic.
- Replies but no closes: the price or the trust signals are wrong.
- Not finishing the delivery inside 7 days: the scope was set too large.
